KAIROSA working research prototype for dynamic prediction of bioprosthetic aortic valve deterioration. Kinetics of Aortic bioprosthesis Integrity, Risk-adapted echO Surveillance; from καιρός, the right moment.

KAIROS demonstrates how valve identity, serial echocardiography, biological susceptibility and treatment history can update the predicted risk of structural valve deterioration while accounting for competing mortality. The prototype extracts real records, develops and evaluates the model on explicitly synthetic scenarios, and lays out the clinical validation that comes next. It does not claim demonstrated clinical accuracy, improved outcomes or deployment readiness.

Summary

Bioprosthetic aortic valves deteriorate, mostly without symptoms, on a timetable that depends on the valve, the patient and the biology between them. Surveillance today is a fixed schedule. KAIROS is a working prototype of the alternative: a dynamic, competing-risk prediction of structural valve deterioration that updates every time an echocardiogram is read and says, for this patient with this valve, how likely deterioration is before death over the next one, three and five years.

The prototype has three layers, and the document keeps them apart throughout.

  • Real-record extraction. Implemented A valve passport is extracted from 202 de-identified operative reports, procedure notes and progress notes covering 117 patients: route, valve model and generation, size, implant year, serial gradients and event mentions. This layer establishes what a hospital record actually contains and how reliably it can be read. It does not train the model.
  • Model development on synthetic scenarios. Demonstrated A penalised cause-specific survival model with dynamic landmarking is developed and evaluated on explicitly synthetic cohorts whose parameters are literature-informed, assumed or varied in sensitivity analysis. Evaluation shows whether the model recovers what the scenarios contain, and whether biomarker and anticoagulant modules add information when it is there and stay quiet when it is not. Synthetic performance is evidence about the demonstration, not about patients.
  • Clinical validation as the next phase. Planned Retrospective development in adequate hospital cohorts, a frozen model, temporal and external validation, then prospective silent evaluation. Only after that would any change to surveillance be tested. Its absence today is the boundary of a 36-hour event, not a failure of the objective.

Two scientific differentiators are carried in full: an organised biomarker panel spanning renal and metabolic, mineral, lipid, cardiac-response and inflammatory-molecular modules, and detailed modelling of anticoagulant exposure as dated, class-specific history. Both are built so the core model runs without them and so their contribution is measured, never assumed.

Problem statement, approach and design decisions

The content of README.md.

Problem statement

Bioprosthetic aortic valves deteriorate, usually without symptoms: in a 1,387-patient surgical cohort about a third developed haemodynamic deterioration under pre-VARC-3 criteria over a decade, and in a 2,403-patient TAVI registry VARC-3 moderate or severe deterioration reached 10.8% at five years. Surveillance follows fixed schedules that differ between guidelines, roughly a third of scheduled five-year echocardiograms in trials never happened, and echo readings vary between sites, so deterioration is often recognised late, when reoperation is urgent rather than planned; in UK national data on all reoperative aortic valve surgery from 1996 to 2019, operative mortality was 4.8% for elective, 11.8% for urgent and 31.7% for emergency cases, a description of how presentation urgency and mortality travel together across all reoperation causes, not a count of preventable deaths. Every bioprosthetic recipient is affected, most acutely younger recipients who will outlive their first valve and for whom valve identity, from withdrawn Trifecta to current SAPIEN 3, changes the odds materially.

Our approach

Data. What a hospital already holds: implant and procedure records with the device identifier, echocardiography reports both structured and free-text, laboratory results, medication history with dates, and outcomes from procedure codes and death records, assembled per patient by text extraction and adjudicated against VARC-3 criteria. Public device identifiers and guideline reference tables supply the device hierarchy and contextual reference values; open-access trial reports and FDA device summaries supply literature-informed simulation parameters.

Method. One primary model: penalised cause-specific proportional hazards for structural valve deterioration and for death, fitted on a landmark dataset so that each prediction uses only information available at that time, and combined into cumulative incidence. Valve identity enters as route and design class, with model and generation where counts allow; partial pooling across sparse devices is a planned extension. Biomarker modules and an anticoagulant-exposure module are added to the core model as separate blocks and judged on incremental calibration and Brier score against a reference model of valve age and type. Gradient boosting is an optional comparison, lower in priority than the modules.

Actionable for surveillance. One inference returns the probability of structural deterioration before death within one, three and five years, conditional on the patient being alive with the index valve and free of deterioration at prediction time, with the corresponding probabilities of death before deterioration and of remaining alive with an intact valve, the main drivers, and a reliability statement. Three separate messages reach the clinic: a current abnormality that needs clinical assessment now, a predicted risk that is high enough to bring forward the next assessment, and a scheduled echo that has not happened. Guideline surveillance stays in place; the model never recommends reintervention or a longer interval.

What is predicted

Predictions condition on the patient being alive, with the index valve in place and free of adjudicated deterioration at the prediction time. Three quantities are reported together; none is labelled as another.

Principal output
Probability of adjudicated structural valve deterioration before death within the prediction horizon: the cause-specific cumulative incidence of SVD at 1, 3 and 5 years from the prediction time.
Companion outputs
Probability of death before deterioration within the horizon, and probability of remaining alive with the index valve and without deterioration. The three sum to one together with the small probability of index-valve replacement for a non-SVD cause, which is reported separately.
Near-term horizon
A 12-month cumulative incidence accompanies every prediction, because the only decision the prototype supports, bringing the next assessment forward, needs a near-term risk, not a five-year one.
Horizons and evidence
One, three and five years are the supported horizons. Eight- and ten-year estimates are exploratory and labelled by whether the device generation's own follow-up reaches them. Individual 15- and 20-year predictions are future research, sketched in Appendix D, and not part of the core demonstration.
Not the complement
One minus the SVD cumulative incidence is not the probability of being alive with a functioning valve; it includes patients who died first. The document never uses it that way.

Study protocol protocol/study_protocol.md

Section numbers follow the submission template. Where a statement describes the prototype rather than the planned study, the status tag says so.

§1 Title and objectives

Title. KAIROS: dynamic prediction of structural deterioration of bioprosthetic aortic valves with competing mortality. Prototype development on synthetic scenarios, with a protocol for retrospective development and independent validation in hospital cohorts.

Primary objective. To develop, and in the next phase externally validate, a dynamic prediction model estimating the 1-, 3- and 5-year cumulative incidence of adjudicated structural valve deterioration before death, conditional on survival and valve status at prediction time, and to quantify the incremental value of serial echocardiography, biomarker modules and anticoagulant-exposure history over patient, valve and reference-echo information.

Secondary objectives. Predict bioprosthetic valve failure, all-cause and SVD-attributable, as a separate outcome; characterise gradient and regurgitation trajectories by route and design class; estimate the association of antithrombotic exposure history with SVD and with thrombosis-related dysfunction as distinct outcomes, reported as predictive associations; test transportability to device generations absent from training where event counts permit.

Exploratory. Simulated counts of patients whose next assessment would be brought forward, under stated assumptions; longer-horizon estimation to 8 and 10 years by device generation; the framework for 15- to 20-year estimation in Appendix D; a nested imaging and pathology component to attribute mechanism where CT or explant data exist.

Out of scope. Any change to guideline surveillance intervals, any treatment recommendation, and any claim of clinical accuracy before validation in real cohorts.

§2 Target population

Time zero. The date of the first adequate transthoracic echocardiogram performed 30 to 180 days after implantation, the qualifying reference study. Patients enter the cohort at that date if they are alive with the index valve and have not met the primary endpoint. Subsequent predictions are made at each later echocardiogram and, for reminder purposes only, at implant anniversaries.

Inclusion. Adults with a first bioprosthetic aortic valve, surgical or transcatheter, implanted at a participating centre, with a qualifying reference study. No criterion depends on what is observed after entry.

Exclusion at entry. Mechanical valves; endocarditis before the reference study; adjudicated deterioration or index-valve replacement before the reference study; concomitant mitral prosthesis. Early procedural failures before the reference study are described separately. Valve-in-valve implants form a pre-specified sensitivity cohort.

Prototype data and dates. Implemented The de-identified records supplied for the event carry year-only dates. They cannot support landmark timing, exposure lags or longitudinal rates. All demonstrations requiring day-level precision use explicitly synthetic dates; the real records contribute extraction findings and descriptive counts only.

Cohort size for the planned study. Planned With valve class and generation categories, spline terms, route-specific baselines and landmark summaries, the core model carries roughly 40 to 60 effective parameters. Riley's criteria with an anticipated Cox-Snell R² of 0.10 imply about 3,400 to 5,100 development patients, with the five-year event fraction taken as 6% and 11% in two planning scenarios; repeated landmark rows add no independent patients or events. External validation needs about 200 primary events for a precise calibration slope. Numbers are recomputed with pmsampsize once endpoint prevalence is measured in the source cohort.

§4 Data sources and ground truth

Implant record and device identifier · echo reports, structured and free-text, for gradients, velocities, area, dimensionless index, regurgitation, LVEF and stroke volume · laboratory results by module (§ Biomarker modules) · medications with start, stop, interruption and switch dates · procedure and diagnosis codes and death records for candidate outcomes · CT or explant findings where they exist. Access through the institutional data warehouse under ethics approval, with text extraction for free text.

Adjudication framework. Candidates are identified from codes and from echo reports flagged by the VARC-3 module; codes never establish a diagnosis. Two reviewers independently classify each candidate using all available evidence, with a third arbitrating. Change-based staging requires the patient's own reference study; published normal values inform context but cannot substitute for the patient's baseline. A stenotic endpoint requires the gradient criterion with the area or dimensionless-index criterion; a regurgitant endpoint requires the regurgitation criterion and is not invalidated by missing area or index. Confirmation is a second study or, where that is impossible, operative, pathological or unequivocal morphological evidence; death or reintervention before a second study is a pre-specified exception. Mechanism is assigned as SVD, thrombosis, endocarditis or non-structural with a confidence grade; where mechanisms overlap, the dominant mechanism is recorded with the alternative, and cases that cannot be resolved are classed uncertain rather than negative. Uncertain cases are excluded from the primary analysis and included in a sensitivity analysis under both classifications.

§5 Statistical analysis plan

Primary model. Penalised cause-specific proportional hazards for SVD, for death and for non-SVD replacement, fitted on a landmark dataset with one row per patient per prediction time, spline terms for continuous predictors, baseline hazards stratified by route, and combined into cumulative incidence. Device effects: design class as fixed effects, with model and generation where at least a pre-specified number of events exist; a frailty term for model nested in class is the planned extension. Demonstrated on synthetic scenarios; Planned in cohorts.

Reference and comparisons. Reference: valve age and valve type. Core: patient, valve, reference echo and serial echo. Core plus biomarker modules; core plus anticoagulant-exposure history; core plus both. Where feasible, core without serial echo, to isolate what updating adds. Every comparison reports calibration-in-the-large and slope, Brier score and its decomposition at 1, 3 and 5 years, with discrimination as supporting evidence, bootstrap uncertainty, and results under missing-data scenarios.

Leakage rules. Only information dated on or before the prediction time; patients who met the endpoint leave the risk set; the echo establishing an endpoint is never a predictor of that event; all rows of one patient stay together in every resampling; preprocessing, imputation and model selection are fitted within training data; the model chosen on development data is frozen before it meets the temporal or external cohort, and no model is selected on the final cohort and then called validated.

Observation process. The primary analysis predicts first adjudicated detection, which can be dated. Sensitivity analyses treat onset as interval-censored between the last negative and first positive study, and model surveillance intensity as informative. Missed visits are a reminder trigger and a confidence penalty; their coefficient, if any, is estimated.

Optional comparison. Planned, lower priority A gradient-boosting survival model fitted to the same cause-specific structure, reported only if it beats the primary model on calibration and Brier score. It is not part of the core demonstration and no other model family is promised.

Validation sequence for the planned study. Develop and tune in designated centres with patient-level resampling; freeze; evaluate in a later implant cohort and an untouched external centre; run leave-one-generation-out checks where events permit. Subgroups: route, design class, manufacturer, age at implant, sex, valve size and mismatch, renal function, diabetes, failure phenotype.

§6 Ethics and privacy

Retrospective, pseudonymised data. In Greece: hospital ethics committee approval, GDPR Article 9(2)(j) research basis, a data protection impact assessment, data minimisation and on-premises training. For a US partner: IRB review with waiver of consent, HIPAA de-identification or a limited dataset under a data use agreement. No identifiable data leaves the institution. Fairness: calibration and discrimination reported by sex, age band, race or ethnicity where recorded, and manufacturer, with recalibration by subgroup if drift is found. Transparency: each output shows the three probabilities, the horizon, the main drivers, the reliability statement and, separately, any reminder; the model never recommends reintervention or treatment changes and never alters guideline surveillance on its own. Reporting follows TRIPOD+AI with a model card.

§1 Formulation

Dynamic prediction by landmarking. The dataset holds one row per patient per prediction time, carrying every feature as known at that time. Cause-specific hazards for SVD, death and non-SVD replacement over the following five years are estimated and combined into cumulative incidence, conditional on being alive with the index valve and SVD-free at the landmark. Thrombosis-related dysfunction is modelled as its own outcome in a secondary analysis, since its predictors (route, intra-annular balloon-expandable design, valve-in-valve, absence of anticoagulation) and its management differ from structural deterioration, and a resolved episode returns the patient to the SVD risk set with the episode recorded as a covariate.

§2 The primary model

Penalised (ridge) cause-specific proportional hazards with splines, route-stratified baselines and landmark time as a covariate. It is interpretable, publishable as a nomogram, and its competing-risk claim is explicit in its structure. Demonstrated on synthetic scenarios in the prototype. Device effects in the prototype are design-class fixed effects with model and generation where the synthetic scenario supplies enough events; the frailty term for partial pooling is planned and described as such wherever the hierarchy is mentioned. A monotonic constraint on gradient change is not imposed: gradients are flow dependent and failure may be regurgitant; a constrained model is a sensitivity analysis, and performance is reported by failure phenotype.

§3 Core features

Static at time zero. Age at implant, sex, BSA and BMI, route, design class, model and generation where available, label size within class, indexed effective orifice area and mismatch grade, tissue treatment, reference mean gradient, dimensionless index, area, regurgitation and paravalvular leak, LVEF and stroke volume index, diabetes with duration, eGFR and dialysis status, smoking status, atrial fibrillation and any other anticoagulation indication, antithrombotic class at time zero, bicuspid native valve, implant year, and for TAVI the residual regurgitation at discharge. Lipid-lowering treatment is recorded as exposure, not as protection.

Longitudinal at each landmark. Current mean gradient, change from the reference study, most recent change and its interval, longer-term slope where three or more studies exist, current and changed dimensionless index and area, regurgitation grade and its change, stroke volume index and LVEF so that flow-dependent gradients are read in context, time since last echo, and the overdue indicator. Only measurements dated on or before the landmark are used.

Missing data. Multiple imputation fitted within training data for covariates that are measured in most patients. Markers essentially absent in a cohort are not imputed into existence: the module that needs them is switched off and the core model reports without it. Stale echo values older than 18 months are flagged, not carried forward.

§4 Outputs

The three probabilities of § What is predicted at 1, 3 and 5 years, the 12-month cumulative incidence, the top drivers, and a reliability statement covering device evidence, data completeness, staleness and, where the model displayed is trained on synthetic scenarios, the label illustrative, unvalidated. Three messages are kept apart: a current abnormality that needs clinical assessment now, which is a finding, not a prediction; a predicted near-term risk high enough to bring the next assessment forward; and an overdue scheduled echo, which is a reminder. A five-year risk never drives an appointment interval.

§5 Clinical integration

Guideline surveillance stays in place and differs by jurisdiction: ESC/EACTS guidance recommends yearly clinical review with imaging for bioprostheses, while the 2020 ACC/AHA guideline considers transthoracic echo at 5 and 10 years and annually thereafter reasonable for surgical bioprostheses and annual echo reasonable after TAVI. The exact wording of the 2025 ESC/EACTS follow-up table was not verified by us Unverified. KAIROS runs when an echo report is finalised and at anniversaries for reminders. It never triggers reintervention, never changes antithrombotic treatment and never lengthens an interval. Whether it improves attendance or finds deterioration earlier is a question for the prospective phase.

§6 Limitations of the model

New and withdrawn devices with short records; sparse or irregular echo and the observation bias it creates; site variability in echo reading; flow-dependent gradients and low-flow states; regurgitant failure that gradients miss; mismatch mistaken for deterioration when the reference is missing; valve-in-valve physiology; extraction errors, above all native versus prosthetic values; confounding by indication and era in every treatment covariate; abrupt events with no preceding signal. The prototype's synthetic training data is the largest limitation and is stated first wherever results appear.

For each, the table states the biological rationale, whether the evidence concerns bioprostheses directly or is extrapolated from native valve or vascular disease, the failure phenotype it speaks to, its availability in the supplied records, how the prototype represents it, and whether its predictive contribution is still hypothetical. No module is turned into a composite score, no marker is assumed to help, and the core model runs when any module is unavailable.

Anticoagulant exposure module

Treatment history is the second differentiator, and it is modelled as dated exposure rather than a discharge checkbox. Its purpose is prediction: to learn whether exposure history carries information about structural deterioration and, separately, about thrombosis-related dysfunction. It makes no treatment recommendation and asserts no causal effect.

What is recorded

  • Drug and class. Vitamin K antagonist, factor Xa inhibitor, direct thrombin inhibitor; antiplatelet agents recorded separately as single or dual therapy.
  • Indication. Atrial fibrillation, venous thromboembolism, post-operative prophylaxis, suspected valve thrombosis, other.
  • History. Start, stop, interruption and switching dates; current status at the prediction time; cumulative prior exposure by class.
  • INR control only where adequately dated INR results exist; year-level data do not qualify.
  • Timing relative to echo findings. An indicator for treatment initiated or changed within a defined window after a suspicious echo, kept as its own variable.
  • Prescription versus adherence. Prescription records are labelled as such; confirmed adherence is a separate field where it exists.

How it is used

Only exposure history available at the prediction time enters the model. Treatment started because dysfunction was suspected creates reverse causation, and a fixed lag alone does not remove the confounding by indication behind it; the module therefore keeps the post-suspicion indicator explicit, reports associations with and without those exposures, and does not apply a blanket lag that would erase recent thrombosis information relevant to the thrombosis outcome. SVD and thrombosis-related dysfunction remain distinct outcomes with distinct feature sets. Pre-specified interactions: antithrombotic class by route, and cumulative vitamin K antagonist exposure by tissue type. Where dp-ucMGP is measured, it enters beside cumulative VKA exposure as the marker of the mechanism the exposure is presumed to act through, so that the model can separate "on warfarin" from "vitamin K depleted". In the supplied records, exposure timelines were reconstructed at year resolution from start, end and discontinuation years for 17 patients Implemented; day-level behaviour is demonstrated on synthetic dates.

How dp-ucMGP enters the model

Dephosphorylated-uncarboxylated matrix Gla protein is a consumed input, not a tracked one. It enters the anticoagulant module as a log-transformed continuous value in pmol/L with the assay recorded, fitted with a spline, through two pre-specified terms: a main effect on the SVD hazard and an interaction with cumulative vitamin K antagonist exposure. The mechanistic hypothesis is directional: if the VKA signal is real it should run through dp-ucMGP, so the VKA coefficient is expected to shrink when the marker is added, and that shrinkage is a reported result. The marker is measured at time zero and at each annual landmark, carried forward for at most twelve months and then marked stale. It is never imputed across a cohort. Instead, the core model with the exposure module is fitted on the full cohort, and in the substudy subset where dp-ucMGP is measured a second model takes the full-cohort linear predictor as an offset and estimates only the dp-ucMGP terms; incremental Brier score and calibration are compared within that subset. In the vitamin K antagonist scenario the generator produces dp-ucMGP from VKA exposure plus noise and gives the SVD hazard its dependence on the marker rather than on the drug, so the demonstration checks that the model attributes the signal to the marker and that the VKA coefficient falls toward zero when the marker is present; a second variant makes the marker pure noise to check the model stays quiet. Every prediction states whether a measured dp-ucMGP value was used.

Evidence, stated in balance

  • Leaflet thrombosis. Oral anticoagulation reduces subclinical leaflet thickening: GALILEO-4D 12.4% on a rivaroxaban strategy versus 32.4% on antiplatelet therapy; ADAPT-TAVR 9.8% on edoxaban versus 18.4% on dual antiplatelet therapy, a difference that did not reach significance; ATLANTIS-4D apixaban comparable to vitamin K antagonists.
  • Clinical outcomes. In GALILEO, the rivaroxaban strategy without an anticoagulation indication increased death or thromboembolic events (HR 1.35), all-cause death (HR 1.69) and major bleeding. In ENVISAGE-TAVI AF, edoxaban was non-inferior to vitamin K antagonists for net adverse events but increased major bleeding (HR 1.40). Reduced imaging-detected thrombosis has not translated into clinical benefit, and factor Xa inhibitors are not described here as neutral or favourable.
  • Calcification. Vitamin K antagonists inhibit matrix Gla protein, an endogenous calcification inhibitor. Observational signals of accelerated deterioration exist (warfarin as a correlate of late deterioration in the Salaun cohort; a small surgical series; higher fluoride uptake on PET in the ANTIPRO substudy Unverified), while other cohorts found anticoagulation associated with less deterioration (Quebec, FRANCE-TAVI) or more (Bern, HR 1.78). The evidence is conflicting and observational.
  • Conclusion for the module. Exposure history is a predictive covariate whose direction may differ by outcome, route and tissue. Nothing here supports switching agents or claims that any anticoagulant prevents structural degeneration.

Simulation design

Synthetic scenarios exist to show whether the model and its modules recover what is in the data and stay honest about what is not. Each parameter is labelled literature-informed, assumed, or varied in sensitivity analysis. Published sub-distribution hazard ratios are not used as cause-specific hazard parameters without conversion and justification. Baseline hazards are anchored, where a curve could be read, to open-access reports (NOTION 10-year) and FDA device summaries; where only point estimates exist, that is stated.

Every synthetic figure is titled as a software demonstration. Synthetic performance is evidence about the demonstration, not clinical validation, and the document never presents it otherwise.

The demonstration

The story is one longitudinal patient. A de-identified record goes in; the valve passport comes out with route, model and generation, size and implant year, and the evidence spans highlighted. The echo trajectory is plotted. The model updates the three probabilities at each landmark, with the drivers and the reliability statement beside them. Where real extracted measurements are shown alongside a model trained on synthetic scenarios, the numerical prediction is labelled illustrative, unvalidated, and the real values appear in the deck only, never in the repository text.

The screen separates the three messages by design: a current abnormality, which is a clinical finding for assessment now; a predicted 12-month risk that would bring the next assessment forward; and an overdue scheduled echo, which is a reminder that needs no model at all. The guideline schedule is displayed beside them and is not modified.

Figures in the deliverable: the pipeline; the passport for one patient; the echo trajectory with landmarks; the three probabilities over time with intervals; calibration and Brier comparisons across the model ladder on synthetic scenarios; module incremental value where information is meaningful, weak and absent; the counts extracted from the real records.

Availability assumptions

  • High risk. Valve model and size coded consistently. In our notes they appear as "#21 Trifecta", "Implant Size: 25" and "Size: Ultra 26mm", so extraction is required.
  • High risk. Serial echo for at least 60% of patients at five years. Published follow-up loss is 30 to 35%.
  • High risk. Lp(a) measured: one test in 17 structured patients. Module switched off where absent.
  • Medium. Mineral-metabolism results outside kidney disease: phosphorus in 4 of 17, PTH in none.
  • Medium. Day-level dates: available in production, absent in the supplied records.
  • Low. Creatinine, HbA1c, lipids and medication history present for essentially all patients.

Preprocessing, labels and governance

Deduplicate by patient, date and measurement; normalise units; parse free text with rules first and an LLM pass second, with an audit sample. Extreme values are flagged for source verification, not rejected. Align every echo to the implant date; define the reference study and the landmarks. Labels come from adjudication, with the VARC-3 module proposing and reviewers deciding. Medication records become dated exposure timelines. The supplied files stay on team machines and out of version control; production would run under a data use agreement, an impact assessment and on-premises compute, with the extraction step audited for leakage of names into structured fields.

Reduced in scope

  • Wearable signals: one sentence in future work (Appendix D). Nothing built.
  • MIMIC-IV-ECHO: a future data-source candidate requiring credentialed access and a feasibility audit of implant identity, serial measurements and outcomes. Not constructed during the event.
  • Individual 15- to 20-year predictions: exploratory future research (Appendix D), not part of the core demonstration.
  • Multiple model families: one primary model; gradient boosting optional.

Limitations and non-claims

  • The model is developed and evaluated on synthetic scenarios. No clinical prediction accuracy is demonstrated, no outcome improvement is shown, and the prototype is not ready for deployment.
  • The supplied records carry year-only dates and almost no serial echo; they establish extraction feasibility and descriptive counts, nothing more.
  • Device effects in the prototype are class-level; hierarchical pooling is planned, not implemented.
  • Biomarker modules beyond the renal and metabolic module are hypothetical in predictive value; several markers are unmeasured in the supplied data and are not imputed.
  • Anticoagulant associations are predictive, confounded by indication and era, and carry no treatment implication.
  • Durability conclusions from randomised trials such as NOTION come from selected, older populations and specific device generations; they inform parameters, not universal claims.
  • Follow-up beyond ten years exists for first-generation TAVI in small surviving cohorts, to about twelve years; contemporary devices have less. Statements about long-horizon TAVI durability are correspondingly limited.
  • Novelty is qualified: to our knowledge no externally validated, individual-level, dynamic prediction model of adjudicated structural valve deterioration is in clinical use; existing work consists of cohort analyses of risk factors and device comparisons.

Continuous covariates: label size within class, indexed effective orifice area, mismatch grade, tissue treatment, implant year. Partial pooling across models within class is planned.

Appendix D. Exploratory future research

  • Long horizons. Older surgical design classes have real 15- to 25-year follow-up in published series; a future analysis could open the surgical implant window to the late 1990s, fit flexible parametric hazards anchored to published long-term curves, backtest extrapolation by censoring at five years and comparing with observed fifteen-year outcomes, and use a multi-state period analysis to estimate lifetime reintervention probability. Individual 15- to 20-year predictions remain research, not output.
  • Wearable triggers. Watch ECG and activity decline as pre-specified triggers for earlier assessment, evaluated for lead time only after independent validation of the core model.
  • Public scale-up. MIMIC-IV-ECHO joined to procedure codes, after credentialing and a feasibility audit.
  • Mechanism. A nested CT and explant component to attribute calcification versus other dysfunction, since rising gradients alone cannot.
