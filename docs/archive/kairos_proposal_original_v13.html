Problem statement, approach and design decisions

These three blocks are the content of README.md.

Problem statement

Bioprosthetic aortic valves wear out, and mostly in silence: about a third of surgical valves show haemodynamic deterioration on echocardiography within a decade and one in ten transcatheter valves by five years, usually before symptoms. Surveillance is one fixed schedule for every patient and valve, a third of five-year echocardiograms never happen, and echo readings vary between sites, so failing valves are found late, when redo aortic surgery carries 11.8% operative mortality if urgent and 31.7% if emergency against 4.8% when elective. Every bioprosthetic recipient is affected, most acutely the growing group implanted under 65, who will outlive their first valve and whose valve identity, from Trifecta to SAPIEN 3, changes the odds materially.

Our approach

Data. Everything a hospital already holds: implant and procedure records with the device identifier, echocardiography reports both structured and free-text, laboratory results, medication history with dates, and outcomes from procedure codes and death records, assembled at patient level by text extraction and adjudicated against VARC-3 criteria. National registries serve external validation and incidence benchmarks. Public device identifiers, guideline reference tables and published durability curves supply the device hierarchy, the reference-value fallback and the scenario parameters.

Method. A dynamic prediction model of adjudicated structural valve deterioration with death as a competing event. The primary model is a penalised cause-specific survival model with dynamic landmarking from a 90-day reference echo, baseline hazards stratified by route, and a hierarchical valve-model effect that pools sparse devices toward their design class. A gradient-boosting survival model is the challenger and earns its place only if it beats the primary model on calibration in frozen evaluation cohorts. The reference is valve age and valve type alone. The choice follows from the data: censored follow-up, competing death, rare events, dozens of sparse valve categories, and clinicians who need to read the model.

Actionable for surveillance. One inference returns the patient's durability curve, the probability of remaining free of adjudicated deterioration at one, three and five years and beyond where the evidence supports it, together with the top drivers, a flag when the patient warrants earlier or additional assessment than the guideline schedule, an overdue-surveillance flag when a scheduled echo has not happened, and a reliability statement that withholds prediction for devices without evidence. Guideline surveillance stays in place for everyone; the model adds attention where risk is, and it never recommends reintervention or lengthens an interval.

Key design decisions

DecisionRationale
Primary endpoint is first adjudicated moderate or severe haemodynamic deterioration attributable to structural deterioration; bioprosthetic valve failure is the key secondary; thrombosis, endocarditis and non-structural dysfunction are classified separately; death is the competing eventReintervention-based endpoints undercount failure and arrive too late to change surveillance. Haemodynamic deterioration is not the same as structural deterioration, so attribution is adjudicated by two reviewers with an "uncertain" class rather than inferred from codes. Death cannot sit inside the endpoint and be the competing event at the same time.
Dynamic landmarking from a 90-day post-operative reference echo, predicting first adjudicated detection, with no eligibility conditioned on future observationUses every patient's available follow-up and re-scores after each echo. Starting at a fixed landmark with information available by that date, and removing patients who already met the endpoint from later risk sets, prevents the model from appearing to predict deterioration that the current echo already shows. Requiring a future echo for inclusion would select patients by what happens after entry.
Valve identity as a three-level hierarchy: route, design class, model and generation, with partial pooling and a withheld prediction below an evidence thresholdModel and generation differences are large and tangled with implant era and patient selection. A frailty term lets a device with few events borrow strength from its class while a device with thousands stands alone. For a new device a class-based estimate is an assumption, so the model says so or says nothing, rather than widening an interval and calling it reliable.
Penalised cause-specific model as primary, gradient boosting as challenger, valve age and type as reference; no automatic class weightingThe primary model implements the competing-risk and pooling claims explicitly instead of implying them; a Cox-objective boosted score alone is a relative hazard, not a competing-risk probability, so the challenger must model death as well. Class weighting distorts absolute risk, and calibration is the metric that decides clinical usefulness.
Irregular echo treated as an observation process: a missed visit raises an overdue flag and lowers confidence, its association with deterioration is learned not imposed, and onset is analysed as interval-censored in sensitivity analysesPatients with symptoms get more echoes and more chances to be detected; missing follow-up may reflect access or care elsewhere. Predictors are tiered by evidence, a core model within the parameter budget plus a tested biomarker extension, and medication exposure enters as dated, class-specific history with a 90-day lag so that a drug started because dysfunction was suspected is never read as its cause.

The study in one screen

Among patients with a bioprosthetic aortic valve, can patient characteristics, prosthesis characteristics and serial echocardiography predict clinically meaningful structural deterioration beyond what valve age and valve type alone predict? KAIROS answers that at a 90-day post-operative landmark and again after each follow-up echo, and tells the clinic which patients need to be looked at sooner.

Durability, defined
The probability that the index valve remains free of adjudicated structural deterioration at t years after the prediction time, given the patient is alive with that valve. Cumulative incidence of deterioration is its complement and the quantity the models estimate.
Horizons
One, three and five years, then 8, 10, 15 and 20 years, each labelled by evidence tier: observed within the device generation's own follow-up; class-based extrapolation where only the design class has follow-up that far; parametric extrapolation beyond that, shown only with a backtesting error attached, or withheld. Every transcatheter horizon beyond ten years is extrapolation, and the output says so.
Primary endpoint
First adjudicated moderate or severe haemodynamic valve deterioration attributable to structural deterioration, by VARC-3 criteria. Bioprosthetic valve failure is the key secondary endpoint with its components reported separately. Death before the primary endpoint is the competing event.
Reference model
Valve age and valve type alone. The guideline surveillance schedule is the standard of care that stays in place, not a comparator for prediction.
What this study does not claim
That surveillance can be reduced. An observational prediction model cannot show that lengthening intervals is safe. Simulated assessments and detection delays are exploratory results under stated assumptions. Changing surveillance is tested only after independent validation and a prospective silent evaluation.
Data reality
Deployment reads implant records, echo reports and outcomes from the EHR. The prototype extracts a valve passport from 215 real de-identified notes and demonstrates the analytical pipeline on synthetic scenarios labelled as software demonstrations, never as evidence of accuracy. Nothing patient-level is committed.
What is new
Not the algorithms. No externally validated prediction model for adjudicated structural deterioration exists, and surveillance is one schedule for everyone. KAIROS adds dynamic updating from serial echo with its incremental value measured against valve age and type, valve identity as a hierarchical input that withholds prediction beyond its evidence, an adjudicated endpoint that separates deterioration from treatment decisions, and a cohort assembled from EHR extraction where registries and imaging datasets cannot supply one. Penalised survival models, gradient boosting, SHAP, VARC-3 staging, competing-risk analysis, the predictor list and LLM extraction are established and are described as such.

Evidence base

Each finding is paired with the design consequence it produced.

  • Deterioration is common and mostly subclinicalSalaun, Circulation 2018: 1,387 surgical bioprostheses; 30.9% developed haemodynamic deterioration under a pre-VARC-3 definition, 3.7% of the cohort within two years; deterioration roughly doubled mortality. Bern TAVI registry, JACC CI 2025: 2,403 patients, VARC-3 moderate or severe deterioration in 2.2% at one year and 10.8% at five, with wide uncertainty at ten, and a near five-fold rise in reintervention.
    EvidenceDecisionPredict adjudicated haemodynamic deterioration attributable to SVD, not reintervention. The Bern five-year figure is one planning scenario; a lower scenario near 6% is planned alongside it.
  • Predictors are known, and they are in the chartEarly surgical deterioration: diabetes, active smoking, renal insufficiency, reference mean gradient of 15 mmHg or more, at least mild regurgitation, stented valves. Late: female sex, warfarin use, valve type. After TAVR: smoking (HR 2.58), diabetes (1.33), kidney disease, BMI, valve size 23 mm or smaller (2.07), severe patient-prosthesis mismatch (1.70), residual regurgitation (1.87), leaflet thrombosis, and intra-annular design in one series. Lipoprotein(a) above 125 nmol/L carried a sub-hazard ratio of 2 to 3 for stenotic deterioration in a 174-patient cohort, while a 97-patient imaging study and a 389-patient reintervention study found no association.
    EvidenceDecisionPredictors are tiered, because a plausible mechanism, an observed association and a validated predictor are different things, and native-valve findings do not transfer automatically to treated animal tissue. Tier A, associated in two or more cohorts: age, valve type and size, mismatch, reference gradient and regurgitation, diabetes, renal dysfunction and dialysis, smoking. Tier B, single-cohort or mixed: Lp(a), metabolic syndrome, phosphate in kidney disease, atherogenic lipids, antithrombotic exposure. Tier C, mechanism only: hs-CRP, fetuin-A, inactive matrix Gla protein, calcification propensity, anti-glycan antibodies. The core model uses Tier A plus routinely available Tier B; the rest is an optional extension tested for incremental value. Lp(a) is analysed continuously, in original units, by failure phenotype.
  • Antithrombotic exposure cuts both ways, and by routeOral anticoagulation reduces and reverses leaflet thrombosis (GALILEO-4D: thickening in 12.4% on rivaroxaban versus 32.4% on antiplatelet therapy; ACASA-TAVI relative risk 0.55), and the effect fades within months of stopping (NOTION-4). On calcification the classes diverge: vitamin K antagonists inhibit matrix Gla protein, warfarin was a correlate of late deterioration in the Salaun cohort, carried a hazard ratio near 2 after three or more months of exposure in a 123-patient surgical series, and raised fluoride uptake on PET (odds ratio 4.3); factor Xa inhibitors are neutral to favourable. Direct comparisons conflict: anticoagulation protective in Quebec (sub-hazard ratio 0.50) and FRANCE-TAVI (0.54), harmful in Bern (1.78); DOAC versus VKA after TAVR no different at four years (0.89). Leaflet thrombosis is two to three times more common after TAVR (13% versus 5% at 30 days in PARTNER 3).
    EvidenceDecisionAntithrombotic exposure enters as history: class at each landmark, cumulative months on a vitamin K antagonist, months since any anticoagulant stopped, the indication, and INR quality where available, with pre-specified interactions by route and tissue type. Thrombosis-attributable dysfunction gets its own cause-specific hazard in a secondary analysis. Drug coefficients are read as predictive, never causal, because indication, kidney disease and era confound them.
  • Valve model and generation differences are large, and confoundedTrifecta versus Perimount Magna Ease: about twenty times the hazard of reoperation for structural deterioration in one series; the FDA warned in February 2023 and Abbott withdrew Trifecta in the US that July, advising lifelong follow-up at least yearly with echo from year one. PARTNER 2 at five years: SVD 9.5% for SAPIEN XT, 3.9% for SAPIEN 3, 3.5% for surgery. CoreValve and SURTAVI: 2.2% versus 4.4%. NOTION at ten years: severe SVD 1.5% versus 10.0%. These comparisons involve different populations, generations, follow-up and endpoints.
    EvidenceDecisionModel manufacturer, model, generation, design class, size and implant era where the data support it. Ignoring either route or model misleads.
  • Surveillance is missed at scaleFive-year echo follow-up was missing in 30.4% of TAVR and 35.7% of surgical patients in a low-risk trial, and only 80% of a 400-patient registry had any follow-up echo. Guidance is a baseline echo within three months, at one year and yearly thereafter, to be verified against the 2025 ESC/EACTS full text.
    EvidenceDecisionDetection depends on observation. A missed visit raises an overdue-surveillance flag and lowers confidence; whether it predicts deterioration is learned and validated, not assumed.
  • Late presentation is costly, by degreeA national registry reported operative mortality for redo aortic surgery of 4.8% elective, 11.8% urgent and 31.7% emergency. Failed valves reaching reintervention in NYHA class IV drive mortality more than the choice of redo surgery versus valve-in-valve. These are associations; they do not prove that surveillance would prevent those deaths.
    EvidenceBriefThe cost-of-late-identification sentence in the problem statement, stated as association.
  • Echo reading is reproducible in expert hands, less so across sitesInter-observer variability of effective orifice area is about 9%; intraclass correlations for gradients and velocities run 0.94 to 0.99 among experts. Core-lab and site readings still diverge, and gradients depend on flow.
    EvidenceDecisionChange relative to each patient's reference echo, gradients read together with dimensionless index, area, regurgitation and stroke volume, and a site effect where identifiable.
  • Free text can be structured reliablyOpen-weight LLMs reached 99.9 to 100% accuracy detecting prosthetic valves and about 99% grading severity from echo reports in a 2025 benchmark. MIMIC-IV-ECHO publishes 206,488 echo studies from 91,372 patients (2008 to 2022) as structured measurements under credentialed access.
    EvidenceDecisionExtraction is the linking technology in the data plan. MIMIC-IV-ECHO is a scale-up candidate pending a feasibility audit of implant histories, device identity, serial prosthetic measurements and outcomes.

Modelling valve type: a hierarchy, without overstating it

The strongest known determinants of deterioration are the valve itself, its size relative to the patient, and its design and generation. Leaving valve identity out would produce a model that is systematically wrong for the patients who most need attention. The case against is real too: dozens of models and generations, sparse categories, newer devices with short follow-up, and era confounding. The design takes both seriously.

  • Partial pooling, implemented. A model with 40 implants and 2 events borrows strength from its class through the frailty term; a model with 2,000 implants stands on its own. Tree features do not do this, and the approach document says so.
  • Unseen or thin devices. Below a pre-specified evidence threshold the model withholds the prediction and says why. A wider interval alone does not establish reliability.
  • Era confounding. In our sample every Trifecta was implanted in 2013 to 2017 and three quarters of SAPIEN valves after 2017. Implant year and landmark time are in the model, and temporal validation trains on earlier implants and tests on later ones.
  • Validation that matches the risk. Leave-one-model-out and leave-one-class-out are transportability checks, run only where event counts permit. Performance is reported by manufacturer, which is also the fairness analysis.
  • Withdrawn devices stay in. Trifecta and Mitroflow patients are alive and under surveillance now, and a withdrawn device is a rule the flag logic applies.
  • A living device prior. Each new device starts at its class prior and earns its own estimate as follow-up accrues. The same machinery could serve post-market surveillance, stated as a possibility, not a result.

What this sample can and cannot carry. It can demonstrate extraction, a valve passport, the VARC-3 classification logic and descriptive statistics on real records. It cannot establish reliable patient-level VARC-3 outcomes or validate durability predictions: about twenty unadjudicated event mentions, year-level dates and almost no serial echo. The prototype therefore demonstrates the pipeline on synthetic scenarios and says so in every file and figure.

Prototype data strategy

  1. Real notes, aggregate outputs. Extraction runs locally; only code, counts and figures without patient-level rows are committed. The .gitignore excludes *.xlsx, data/raw/ and data/derived/private/.
  2. Synthetic scenarios, not a synthetic cohort. Several simulated datasets that vary failure mechanism mix, competing mortality, follow-up intensity and informativeness, and device effects, with parameters in one YAML file, each parameter carrying its source, definition and confidence. They exercise extraction interfaces, censoring, competing-risk calculations and evaluation code. Baseline hazards are anchored where possible to curves reconstructed from the open-access NOTION report and the FDA device summaries; reconstructed rows are labelled reconstructed, never real, and synthetic performance is never presented as evidence of clinical accuracy.
  3. Scale-up candidate, not built. MIMIC-IV-ECHO, joined to MIMIC-IV procedure codes, is the first item of the three-month plan, pending credentialed access and a feasibility audit.

Study protocol protocol/study_protocol.md

The main deliverable. Section numbers follow the template; the physician owns the clinical wording.

§1 Title and objectives

Title. KAIROS: a dynamic prediction model of bioprosthetic aortic valve durability. Retrospective development, independent temporal and external validation.

Definition. Durability is the probability that the index valve remains free of adjudicated structural deterioration at t years after the prediction time, given the patient is alive with that valve. Horizons are one, three and five years, extending to eight, ten, fifteen and twenty wherever the evidence tier allows. Cumulative incidence of deterioration is its complement and is the quantity the models estimate.

Primary objective. To develop and externally validate a dynamic prediction model estimating the one-, three- and five-year cumulative incidence of adjudicated structural deterioration of bioprosthetic aortic valves, conditional on survival and valve status at prediction, accounting for competing mortality and evaluating the incremental value of serial echocardiography over baseline patient and prosthesis characteristics.

Secondary objectives. Quantify the incremental value of the 90-day reference echo and of each subsequent echo over valve age and type alone; characterise gradient and regurgitation trajectories by route and design class; predict bioprosthetic valve failure and SVD-related reintervention as separate competing-risk outcomes; test transportability to valve models absent from training where event counts permit; estimate durability to 20 years by evidence tier, with the extrapolation method backtested on the older surgical cohort. Exploratory. Simulate how many patients the model would flag for earlier or additional assessment and the associated detection lead time, under stated assumptions; test whether an optional biomarker extension adds calibration or discrimination beyond the core model; run a nested imaging and pathology component, adjudicated leaflet calcification on CT where available and explant findings, to attribute mechanism; evaluate pre-specified wearable triggers, watch ECG and activity decline, for lead time between scheduled echoes. Out of scope. Any change to guideline surveillance intervals, which belongs to a later prospective study.

§2 Target population

Landmark. The main model starts at a 90-day post-operative landmark using information available by that date and a reference transthoracic echo obtained within 30 to 180 days, the study closest to day 90. Estimates are prognoses among patients alive with their index valve at that landmark. Later landmarks are each subsequent echo and each implant anniversary.

Inclusion. Adults with a first bioprosthetic aortic valve, surgical or transcatheter, implanted 2005 to 2023 at a participating centre, alive with the index valve at day 90, with a reference echo in the window. No eligibility criterion depends on what is observed after entry. For the long-horizon analysis the surgical implant window opens at 1998 so that the older design classes contribute real 15- to 25-year follow-up; the added era confounding is handled by implant year as a covariate and era-stratified baseline hazards.

Exclusion. Mechanical valves; endocarditis or valve thrombosis before the landmark; concomitant mitral prosthesis. Deaths, reinterventions and failures before day 90 are described separately as early procedural outcomes. Valve-in-valve implants form a pre-specified sensitivity cohort.

Cohort size. The intended model carries valve class and generation categories, spline terms, route-specific baselines and landmark summaries, plausibly 40 to 60 effective parameters. Under Riley's criteria with a Cox-Snell R² of 0.10, that implies roughly 3,400 to 5,100 development patients, with the five-year event fraction taken as 6% and 11% in two scenarios; repeated landmark rows add no independent patients or events. External validation is planned separately and needs about 200 primary events for a precise calibration slope. Final numbers are recomputed with pmsampsize once endpoint prevalence is verified in the source data.

§4 Data sources and ground truth

Implant record and device identifier (valve model, size, route, date) · echo reports, structured and free-text, for gradients, velocities, area, dimensionless index, regurgitation, LVEF, stroke volume · laboratory results for creatinine, HbA1c, lipids, Lp(a), NT-proBNP and mineral metabolism · medications with dates for antithrombotic, lipid-lowering, glucose-lowering and mineral-metabolism classes · procedure and diagnosis codes and death records for outcomes · optionally CT for leaflet thickening. Access through the institutional data warehouse under ethics approval, with extraction for free text.

Adjudication. Two independent reviewers integrate all available evidence for each candidate case, with arbitration by a third. Procedure and diagnosis codes identify candidates; they never establish the diagnosis. Evidence is weighed in order: explant pathology or operative findings; reintervention records with documented mechanism; VARC-3 echo criteria against the patient's reference echo, confirmed on a second study; morphology and clinical context. Pre-specified exceptions to the two-study confirmation: death or reintervention before a second study, operative or pathological confirmation, or unequivocal morphology. Missing area or dimensionless index, or inadequate imaging, produces an uncertain classification, never an automatic negative. Thrombosis, flow changes, pannus and measurement variability are excluded before attribution, and baseline mismatch is not deterioration.

§5 Statistical analysis plan

Primary model. Penalised cause-specific proportional hazards with dynamic landmarking, spline terms for nonlinear effects, baseline hazards stratified by route, a frailty term for valve model nested in design class, and death modelled as its own cause-specific hazard. Cumulative incidence is obtained by combining the cause-specific hazards. Pre-specified interactions. Antithrombotic class by route, and cumulative vitamin K antagonist exposure by tissue type. Drug coefficients are predictive, not causal: an active-comparator sensitivity analysis restricted to anticoagulated patients and a second analysis excluding patients with an anticoagulation indication test whether they reflect the drug or the indication. Challenger. Gradient boosting fitted to both event processes and combined to cumulative incidence. Reference. Valve age and valve type alone. No automatic class weighting.

Leakage and timing rules. At every landmark, only information available at that date; patients who already met the endpoint leave the risk set; all records of one patient stay together in every resampling. Medication exposures count only if started at least 90 days before the landmark, and a drug begun after a suspicious echo, above all anticoagulation for suspected leaflet thrombosis, is coded as a consequence of dysfunction. Core model and extension. The core model is developed and frozen first; the biomarker extension is judged only on incremental calibration and discrimination in the frozen evaluation cohorts. Observation process. The primary analysis predicts first adjudicated detection; sensitivity analyses treat onset as interval-censored between the last negative and first positive study and model informative observation.

Long-horizon method validation. Parametric cause-specific hazards (Weibull, Gompertz, log-logistic and Royston-Parmar splines) are fitted with shapes constrained to a hazard that rises with valve age and falls with patient age, anchored to the published 20-year surgical curves through priors on the shape parameters. The method is backtested on the older surgical cohort by censoring follow-up at five years, extrapolating to fifteen and comparing with what was observed; the error measured there is the uncertainty attached to every extrapolation for newer devices. A multi-state period analysis (normal function, stage 1, stage 2, stage 3, failure, death) estimates valve-age-specific transition hazards from every patient at risk at each valve age regardless of implant year, and yields the lifetime probability of reintervention and the probability that the valve outlives the patient.

Validation sequence. Develop and tune in designated centres with patient-level resampling; freeze the model; evaluate in a pre-specified later implant cohort and in an untouched external centre; run leave-one-model-out and leave-one-class-out checks where event counts permit. Metrics, in priority order. Calibration (calibration-in-the-large, slope, integrated calibration index), Brier score, time-dependent AUC and C-index at supported horizons, decision-curve net benefit, all with bootstrap intervals. Subgroups. Route, design class, manufacturer, age at implant, sex, valve size and mismatch, renal function, diabetes; failure phenotype (stenotic, regurgitant, mixed).

§6 Ethics and privacy

Retrospective, pseudonymised data. In Greece: hospital ethics committee approval, GDPR Article 9(2)(j) research basis, a data protection impact assessment, data minimisation and on-premises training. For a US partner: IRB review with waiver of consent, HIPAA de-identification or a limited dataset under a data use agreement. No identifiable data leaves the institution; inference runs inside the EHR. Fairness: discrimination and calibration reported by sex, age band, race or ethnicity where recorded, and manufacturer, with recalibration by subgroup if drift is found. Transparency: each output shows the durability curve with its evidence tiers, top drivers, an overdue-surveillance flag where relevant and a reliability statement that may withhold the prediction; the model never recommends reintervention and never shortens or lengthens guideline surveillance on its own; reporting follows TRIPOD+AI with a model card.

§1 Formulation

Dynamic prediction by landmarking: a stacked dataset with one row per patient per landmark, predicting the cause-specific hazards of adjudicated deterioration and of death over the following five years and combining them into cumulative incidence conditional on being alive with the index valve at the landmark. A secondary analysis separates thrombosis-attributable dysfunction as a third cause-specific process, since its predictors (route, intra-annular balloon-expandable design, valve-in-valve, absence of anticoagulation) and its management differ from structural deterioration. This uses every patient's available observation time, re-scores after each echo, and separates baseline prognosis at day 90 from identification of accelerating deterioration later. The outcome predicted is first adjudicated detection; the difference between detection and onset is handled in sensitivity analyses. Survival analysis does not remove the information shortage of rare events; it only uses them correctly.

§2 Models

Primary. Penalised cause-specific survival model with dynamic landmarking, spline terms, route-stratified baselines and a valve-model frailty nested in design class. Interpretable, publishable as a nomogram, and it implements both the competing-risk and the partial-pooling claims rather than implying them.

Challenger. Gradient-boosted survival trees fitted to both event processes and combined to cumulative incidence, with SHAP for per-prediction drivers. It earns its place only if it beats the primary model on calibration and Brier score in the frozen evaluation, not on discrimination alone. Valve categories enter as plain features and no pooling is claimed for it.

Curve display. A flexible parametric cause-specific model on Royston-Parmar splines produces the smooth curve shown to clinicians, with a line-style change where observed data end and extrapolation begins.

Reference. Valve age and valve type alone.

Monotonic constraints. A monotonic constraint on gradient change would require that, holding every other input constant, a larger gradient increase cannot lower the predicted hazard. It does not impose a monotonic post-operative trajectory. Its suitability is nonetheless uncertain, because gradients are flow dependent and structural failure may present predominantly with regurgitation. The primary model is therefore unconstrained and carries the longitudinal haemodynamic features below; a constrained model is a pre-specified sensitivity analysis. Performance is assessed separately across stenotic, regurgitant and mixed phenotypes. Recognising an abrupt abnormality once it appears is different from predicting it beforehand, and no model can anticipate a sudden leaflet tear if the preceding measurements carry no signal.

§3 Features

Core model, static at day 90. Age at implant, sex, BSA and BMI, route, design class, model and generation, label size within class, indexed effective orifice area and mismatch grade, tissue treatment, reference mean gradient, dimensionless index, area, regurgitation and paravalvular leak, LVEF and stroke volume index, diabetes type and duration with HbA1c, eGFR with dialysis status and start date, smoking status with pack-years and cessation date, LDL and non-HDL cholesterol with lipid-lowering treatment recorded as exposure and not as protection, atrial fibrillation and any other anticoagulation indication, antithrombotic class at the landmark (vitamin K antagonist, factor Xa or direct thrombin inhibitor, single or dual antiplatelet, none), bicuspid native valve, implant year, and for TAVR the residual regurgitation at discharge and calcium volume where CT exists. The core model stays inside the effective-parameter budget; the metabolic syndrome label and all its components are not entered together.

Biomarker extension, optional. Lp(a) in original units with assay and date; phosphate, albumin-corrected and ionised calcium, PTH and alkaline phosphatase, chiefly meaningful in kidney disease; hs-CRP during clinical stability; and, in a molecular substudy, Lp-PLA2, circulating PCSK9 and oxidised-lipid measures, anti-alpha-Gal and anti-Neu5Gc IgG for animal-derived tissue, fetuin-A, inactive matrix Gla protein and serum calcification propensity. Mineral-metabolism drugs and glucose-lowering classes enter as exposures and markers of disease severity, never as anticalcification treatments. Normal serum calcium says nothing about leaflet calcification, and vascular or native-valve evidence for these markers is labelled extrapolation. The extension is evaluated only for incremental calibration and discrimination over the frozen core model.

Longitudinal, at each landmark. Current mean gradient; change from the reference echo; most recent change and the interval over which it occurred; longer-term slope where three or more studies exist; current and changed dimensionless index and area; regurgitation grade and its change; stroke volume index and LVEF, so that flow-dependent gradients are read in context; NT-proBNP trend; metabolic and mineral trajectories, meaning eGFR slope and any dialysis start, HbA1c trajectory, BMI trajectory, smoking status changes, and phosphate and corrected calcium where measured; antithrombotic exposure history, meaning current class, cumulative months on a vitamin K antagonist since implant, months since any anticoagulant was stopped, and INR time in therapeutic range where INR results exist; new atrial fibrillation or heart-failure admission; time since last echo; and the overdue-surveillance indicator. Every summary uses only measurements dated before the landmark.

Missing data and observation. Multiple imputation in the primary model; native handling with indicators in the challenger. Gradients older than 18 months are marked stale, not carried forward. A missed visit raises the overdue flag and reduces confidence in the current estimate; whether it predicts deterioration is learned from the data and validated, because missing follow-up may reflect access, care elsewhere or scheduling.

§4 Validation

As in protocol §5: develop, freeze, evaluate in a later cohort and an untouched external centre, then transportability checks. In the prototype: patient-grouped cross-validation on synthetic scenarios, a temporal split and a leave-one-class-out run to exercise the code paths. The notebook prints every metric with bootstrap intervals and labels every result as synthetic.

§5 Outputs

One inference returns the predicted durability: the probability of remaining free of adjudicated deterioration at one, three and five years, and at 8, 10, 15 and 20 years each labelled by its evidence tier, conditional on survival and valve status now, drawn as a curve whose line style changes where observed data end, with cumulative incidence available as its complement; the top three drivers; a flag when the patient warrants earlier or additional assessment relative to the guideline schedule; and a reliability statement that may withhold the prediction for a device or context without adequate evidence. When current findings are already abnormal, symptoms are new or regurgitation is significant, the output is a diagnostic-assessment pathway, not a future-risk tier. The pathway is mechanism-aware: a thrombosis-dominant pattern, for instance an unanticoagulated intra-annular transcatheter valve with a rising gradient within two years, names CT for leaflet thickening and an anticoagulation review, because that mechanism is reversible; a structural-dominant pattern goes to the structural heart team. Short-term risk and current findings, not five-year risk, drive any decision between a six- and a twelve-month appointment, and that decision stays with the clinician. At the day-90 landmark the model can show expected deterioration by valve class for a patient with this profile, labelled descriptive and not causal.

§6 Clinical integration

Guideline surveillance stays in place for every patient: a baseline echo within three months, at one year and yearly thereafter, to be verified against the 2025 ESC/EACTS full text. KAIROS runs passively when an echo report is finalised, when an implant anniversary passes with no echo, and on request. It adds two things: a flag for patients who need earlier or additional assessment, and an overdue-surveillance flag for patients who have dropped out of the schedule. Results land on the valve-clinic worklist; a nurse coordinator books assessments; the structural heart team reviews flags. The model never triggers a reintervention referral and never lengthens an interval. Whether it improves attendance, finds deterioration earlier or could safely change intervals is tested only after independent validation and a prospective silent evaluation.

§7 Limitations

New valve models and withdrawn devices with short records; sparse or irregular echo and the observation bias it creates; reader and site variability; flow-dependent gradients and low-flow states; regurgitant failure that gradients miss; mismatch mistaken for deterioration when the reference is missing; valve-in-valve physiology; extraction errors, above all native versus prosthetic values and unit slips; confounding by indication and by era in every drug covariate, and exposure misclassification from prescription records; year-level dates in our sample; and abrupt events with no preceding signal. Clinicians see these as the reliability statement and a plain-language reason. The synthetic data of the prototype is itself the largest limitation and is stated first.

Future inputs, not built: watch ECG and activity

Two wearable signals are named in the protocol as an exploratory objective, in approach §3 as future inputs and in approach §6 as trigger tiers, and nothing is built for them in this submission. Their role would be as pre-specified triggers for earlier assessment: a positive AI-ECG for structural heart disease, or a sustained within-person decline in activity. Their accuracy contribution is between echoes, when the last gradient is a year or more old, so gains would appear as lead time rather than as a higher five-year AUC. The ECG component can be developed retrospectively from twelve-lead ECGs already in the record, since lead I approximates a watch lead and every echo report supplies a label. Activity mainly informs the competing death hazard. Wearable terms would enter the deterioration model only after about 100 events have been observed in exposed patients, a rule to pre-register, and protocol echoes stay on the fixed schedule in any validation cohort so that trigger-driven echoes do not bias labels.

Availability assumptions

  • High risk. Valve model and size are coded consistently. In our notes they appear as "#21 Trifecta", "Implant Size: 25" and "Size: Ultra 26mm", so extraction is required and a device-identifier field cannot be assumed.
  • High risk. Serial echo exists for at least 60% of patients at five years. Published follow-up loss is 30 to 35%, and our sample shows most patients with a single year of notes.
  • High risk. Lp(a) is measured. One test in 17 structured patients. Part of the optional extension, in original units with a missingness indicator.
  • Medium. Mineral-metabolism results exist outside kidney disease. Phosphorus appears for 4 of 17 structured patients and PTH for none, so these belong to the extension.
  • Medium. Echo report text carries prosthetic values distinct from native values; the extractor needs context rules to separate them.
  • Medium. Dates are available at day resolution in production; our sample has years only, which is why the prototype's time axis is synthetic.
  • Low. Creatinine, HbA1c, lipids and medication history are present for essentially all patients.

Preprocessing and labels

Deduplicate by patient, date and measurement; normalise units; parse free text with the rule set first and an LLM pass second, with the pilot audit and later an enriched annotated sample. Extreme values, such as gradients above 60 mmHg or areas below 0.6 cm², are not rejected: they may be severe dysfunction and are flagged for source verification. Align every echo to the implant date, define the reference echo and the landmark windows, and take the latest echo within each window as the index value. Medication records are mapped from RxNorm to class and assembled into a per-patient exposure timeline. Labels come from adjudication, not from rules alone: the VARC-3 module proposes a stage against the reference echo and the reviewers confirm, reclassify or mark uncertain. Event prevalence is reported by class and by phenotype.

Synthetic scenarios

The cohort a real study assembles spans changes in prosthesis design, patient selection and follow-up practice; in our own sample implantation years and device distribution were verified first, and they confound era with device. A synthetic dataset parameterised from a small number of published hazard ratios cannot establish representativeness across these settings, particularly when the source studies use different endpoints and populations. Simulation is therefore limited to demonstrating the analytical pipeline and testing robustness under explicitly varied assumptions: failure mechanism mix, competing mortality, follow-up intensity and informativeness, and device effects. One scenario gives a vitamin K antagonist a higher calcific hazard and any anticoagulant a lower thrombosis hazard, to check that the multi-cause structure recovers opposite-direction effects rather than averaging them away. Synthetic performance must not be interpreted as evidence of clinical accuracy or of transportability to contemporary valve recipients.

Governance

The three files were provided by the organisers for the event; they stay on team machines, are excluded from version control, and only aggregates appear in the repository. Placeholders such as [NAME] and [DATE] show they were de-identified at source; publishing counts adds no re-identification risk. Production would run under a data use agreement, an impact assessment and on-premises compute, with the extraction step audited for leakage of names into structured fields.
